# Annual suitability review: Rosalind Teague

**From:** Owen Castellan, Merrow Lane Wealth Management Ltd, Bristol · **To:** Claire Mabey, attorney for Rosalind Teague · **Date:** 12 November 2026 · **Reference:** SR-2026-0418

Dear Ms Mabey,

Thank you for meeting me at our Bristol office on 29 October 2026 for your mother's annual review. You now act for Rosalind Teague under the lasting power of attorney registered in February, so this letter, and every review from now on, comes to you. It sets out what changed this year, what we did about it, and why we believe her investments remain suitable.

## Our conclusion

Both of Mrs Teague's accounts remain suitable for her. At the review we agreed that her attitude to risk is now cautious rather than balanced. She moved into Hallowmere House in Wells in August; the fees are £5,850 a month, her state and teachers' pensions meet £3,850 of that, and her pension pot has to meet the rest for at least eight years. The changes below bring the portfolios into line with that profile.

## What we changed this year

The Drawdown Decision, recorded on 4 August 2026, was recommended by Graham Pitt shortly before he retired from the firm. The Drawdown Decision applied the Sustainable Withdrawal Policy and set Mrs Teague's income from the pension at £2,000 a month from September.

Hallowmere House asked for a deposit of £22,000 before Mrs Teague moved in. The Care Deposit Waiver, which Sunil Varma signed off on 15 August, led to TX-70602, a single payment from the pension. TX-70602 breached the Sustainable Withdrawal Policy: it took this year's withdrawals to 12.7% of the pension, against a ceiling of 8%. You agreed the payment in writing beforehand, and the waiver records why it was in Mrs Teague's interest.

The De-risking Decision was preceded by the Drawdown Decision and was recommended by Nadia Brooke after we reassessed Mrs Teague's attitude to risk on 7 October. It applied our Client Suitability Policy, which requires a fresh assessment whenever a client's circumstances change. The De-risking Decision led to TX-70815 and TX-70816, which moved £58,000 out of smaller companies and into short-dated bonds. TX-70816 bought £58,000 of the Calder Short-Dated Bond Fund (ISIN GB00B7Q2LM41), which was issued by Calder Fund Managers Ltd and holds sterling bonds that mature within five years.

ACC-6107 does not hold the Wrenfield UK Smaller Companies Fund, so the ISA needed no change. Our email of 20 August said that TX-70602 also breached the Client Suitability Policy; that was wrong, and the file has been corrected.
