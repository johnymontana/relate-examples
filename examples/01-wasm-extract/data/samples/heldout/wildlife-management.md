# Field incident report: snared elephant cow, Ndarasi Game Reserve

**From:** Corporal Esther Wambui, patrol leader · **To:** Senior Warden · **Date:** 15 October 2026 · **Reference:** NGR-INC-2026-047

## Summary

At 07:40 on 14 October, PTL-1146 found Naipanoi, an eleven-year-old elephant cow, with a wire snare cutting into her right foreleg at the edge of the seasonal swamp in the north-west block. The Kenya Wildlife Service mobile veterinary unit darted her at 11:15, cut the snare off and treated the wound, and she rejoined her family by 13:00. The patrol lifted 23 more snares and found a bushmeat camp within 2 km of the swamp. No arrests have been made.

## The reserve and the patrol

Ndarasi Game Reserve covers 312 km² of Taita-Taveta County. Ndarasi Game Reserve contains acacia-commiphora bushland on the ridges and a seasonal swamp that holds water until December, the only dry-season water in the north-west block.

Esther Wambui is stationed at Ndarasi Game Reserve and has led its north-west foot patrols since 2024. PTL-1146, a foot patrol of six rangers led by Corporal Esther Wambui, covered the north-west block of Ndarasi Game Reserve from 05:30 on 14 October. Ranger Kevin Mwakio, stationed at Ndarasi Game Reserve as its radio operator, kept the patrol in contact with headquarters and called out the veterinary unit.

SG-7698 was captured by CT-22, a camera trap set in seasonal swamp on the main elephant path, three days before the patrol found her. CT-22 is one of nine camera traps deployed in Ndarasi Game Reserve. SG-7702 was reported by Esther Wambui at 07:40 and identified Naipanoi by the V-shaped tear in her left ear.

Naipanoi is an elephant of the Lenjo family, whose matriarch is the oldest cow in the reserve. Naipanoi lives in Ndarasi Game Reserve all year. In the dry season the elephant inhabits the seasonal swamp almost every night, which is why the snare line was set there.

## What the patrol found

PTL-1146 found evidence of snaring along 1.5 km of the swamp edge: 23 cable snares set at head height on the game paths, most of them cut from fencing wire. Here snaring threatens elephant as much as the smaller antelope the snares are set for, because a wire round a foreleg cuts deeper each day an elephant walks on it.

The patrol also found evidence of bushmeat poaching: a camp with a drying rack, fresh ashes and the skin of a young antelope. In this block, bushmeat poaching threatens lesser kudu and dik-dik most, and the camp's position shows that whoever set it knew where the swamp draws them.

## Follow-up

PTL-1150, led by Ranger Kevin Mwakio on 15 October, swept the same block and found no further evidence of snaring. SG-7695, from CT-22 on 9 October, may show Naipanoi already limping, but the frame is too dark to be sure. CT-25 will be deployed in Ndarasi Game Reserve at the camp site next week, facing the path the poachers used.

## Actions requested

1. Run a daily foot patrol of the swamp edge until the short rains fill the river.
2. Ask the veterinary unit to check Naipanoi's wound again on 18 and 25 October.
3. Pass the camp's location and the snare wire to the police post for the investigation file.
4. Meet the village elders on the reserve's western boundary about the fencing wire.
