# Hearing preparation note: the Stay Hearing in Ellery v Brannock

**From:** Megan Pryor, Dunmore Rees LLP · **To:** Harriet Osei, Quill Street Chambers · **Date:** 1 December 2026 · **Claim number:** CL-2026-000377

## The hearing

The Stay Hearing is listed in Ellery v Brannock for 8 December 2026, with a time estimate of half a day. The Stay Hearing is before Mr Justice Calloway, who has read the papers and asked for skeleton arguments of no more than fifteen pages. It will determine the Stay Application, and nothing else is listed with it.

## The case

Ellery Foods Ltd is the claimant in Ellery Foods Ltd v Brannock Packaging Ltd, which we call Ellery v Brannock. Brannock Packaging Ltd, our client, is the defendant. Ellery v Brannock is proceeding in the Commercial Court. The Claim Form was issued on 14 August 2026 under claim number CL-2026-000377, and Ellery served the Particulars of Claim with it.

Ellery v Brannock arises under the Supply Agreement dated 1 September 2023, which Ellery Foods Ltd and Brannock Packaging Ltd signed for the supply of printed barrier film to Ellery's ready-meal factory in Wakefield. Ellery says that film delivered between January and May 2026 failed its oxygen-barrier specification, and claims £1.36 million for spoiled stock and a lost retail contract.

## Who acts

Dunmore Rees LLP acts for Brannock. Harriet Osei, who represents Brannock on the application, will appear at the Stay Hearing with me. Castlegate Hume LLP acts for Ellery. Ravi Chaudhry, of Tallis Yard Chambers, represents Ellery Foods Ltd.

## The application

The Stay Application was filed by Brannock Packaging Ltd on 21 October 2026, before any step in the action, as section 9 of the Arbitration Act 1996 requires. The Stay Application cites the Arbitration Act 1996 and asks the court to stay the whole claim in favour of arbitration in London. The Stay Application relies on clause 19 of the Supply Agreement, which refers "any dispute arising out of or in connection with this agreement" to arbitration under the LCIA Rules.

## Ellery's arguments and our answers

1. **A later contract.** Ellery alleges that Brannock Packaging Ltd signed a Framework Agreement in March 2025 that replaced clause 19 with a clause giving the English courts exclusive jurisdiction. Our client has searched its records and found only an unsigned draft, which Brannock's commercial director rejected by email on 14 March 2025.
2. **Scope.** Ellery says the claim for the lost retail contract falls outside clause 19. The words "in connection with" are wide, and the court will give them their natural meaning unless the parties clearly intended otherwise.
3. **Waiver.** The Particulars of Claim do not mention clause 19, and Ellery says that Brannock waived arbitration by corresponding about settlement. Correspondence of that kind is not a step in the action.
