# Regulatory change impact assessment RCA-2026-017: the K-DTF amendment to MIFIDPRU 4.12

**To:** Prudential Risk and Capital Committee · **From:** Regulatory Change Office · **Date:** 8 June 2026 · **Reference:** RCA-2026-017 · **Classification:** internal

## Summary

The FCA has amended MIFIDPRU 4.12 (K-DTF daily trading flow requirement), the section that sets the own funds requirement for the daily trading flow of a firm dealing on its own account. The amended text takes effect on 15 July 2026. A new subsection, MIFIDPRU 4.12.1 (Measurement of daily trading flow), sits within MIFIDPRU 4.12 and takes effect two weeks earlier, on 30 June 2026.

MIFIDPRU 4.12 is the most heavily cited section in our map of the Handbook. Seven sections cite it directly, from all three sourcebooks we map: MIFIDPRU, SYSC and COBS. Followed three citations out, the amendment reaches 13 sections beyond the amended text, owned by six teams. We ask the Committee to approve that scope and the confirmation dates in the action table below.

## Where the amended text sits

MIFIDPRU 4.12 sits within MIFIDPRU 4 (Own funds requirements). MIFIDPRU 4 is one of nine chapters that sit directly beneath MIFIDPRU, the prudential sourcebook for investment firms. The amendment changes how daily trading flow is measured and moves the detailed measurement rules into the new subsection, so a citation to MIFIDPRU 4.12 can now land on text that did not exist when the citing rule was written.

MIFIDPRU 4.1, which also sits within MIFIDPRU 4, requires the management body to approve the K-DTF calculation each year. MIFIDPRU 1.2.1 sits within MIFIDPRU 1.2 (Notification requirements), and its notification trigger is the breach test in MIFIDPRU 4.12.5R.

Two of the seven direct citations cross into other sourcebooks, and they are the ones a review scoped to MIFIDPRU would miss. SYSC 7.3, in SYSC 7 (Risk control), cites MIFIDPRU 4.12 to set the point at which the risk control function must escalate a K-DTF breach. COBS 11.2 cites MIFIDPRU 4.12 for the dealing limits that apply while a firm moves to the new measure. COBS 11.2 sits within COBS 11 (Dealing and managing), which sits directly beneath COBS.

## Second and third hops

Three sections reach the amendment through one intermediate citation. COBS 11.4 (Application), also in COBS 11, cites SYSC 7.3. That single citation carries a MIFIDPRU change into the COBS rules on dealing by way of SYSC, across two sourcebook boundaries.

COBS 6.2 (Governance and oversight) is reached by two routes. It cites both COBS 11.2 and COBS 11.4.

COBS 11.4 is itself cited by two more sections of COBS, which therefore sit three citations from the amendment:

- COBS 16A.2 (Calculation), in the chapter on reporting to clients.
- COBS 9A.3 (Application), in the suitability chapter.

On the MIFIDPRU side, MIFIDPRU 8.3 (Exemptions) cites MIFIDPRU 6.2, the record-keeping section. MIFIDPRU 8.3 is in turn cited by MIFIDPRU 9.3 (Reporting timetable), the third hop. MIFIDPRU 9.3 and MIFIDPRU 9.2 both sit within MIFIDPRU 9 (Regulatory reporting), so the regulatory reporting team will review them together.

## Points we could not confirm

We checked the following candidate dependencies and have left them out of the count:

- MIFIDPRU 4.2 does not cite MIFIDPRU 4.12, and the exemptions it grants are unaffected.
- The trading desk believes COBS 11.1 may also rely on MIFIDPRU 4.12, but we found that reference only in a desk procedure.
- A vendor's mapping claims that SYSC 8.3 cites MIFIDPRU 4.12.1; the published text of SYSC 8.3 holds no such reference.
- Under the FCA's proposed text, COBS 16A.1 would cite MIFIDPRU 4.12.1 once the 2027 reporting changes are made.
- The proposed text would also retitle MIFIDPRU 4.12.1 as Measurement and reporting of daily trading flow.
- An earlier draft of this assessment placed MIFIDPRU 4.12.1 within MIFIDPRU 4.1; that was an error and has been corrected.

## Recommendation

Approve the 13-section scope and the confirmation dates above. Treat the 13 as a floor rather than a ceiling: the map holds only the cross-references we have captured, and a citation we have missed would widen the impact. We will re-run the dependency map when the FCA publishes the final instrument and report any change to the Committee at its July meeting.
