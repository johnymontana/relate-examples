# Compliance memo: issuer concentration in ACC-2207

**From:** Dana Whitlock, Compliance · **To:** Investment Committee, Halvorsen Pryce Partners LLP · **Date:** 6 October 2026 · **Reference:** CM-2026-031

## Summary

On 22 September 2026, TX-4471 took account ACC-2207 over the firm's single-issuer limit. TX-4471 breached the Concentration Limit Policy, which caps exposure to any one issuer at 10% of an account's value. The breach lasted eleven trading days and was closed on 7 October. No client lost money and the position remained suitable, so we do not consider the breach reportable. The way it happened, however, shows a gap in the pre-trade checks that the committee should close this quarter.

## The firm and the people

Halvorsen Pryce Partners LLP is an Edinburgh wealth manager, firm reference number 482910. Halvorsen Pryce Partners LLP is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Colm Brady works for Halvorsen Pryce Partners LLP as a director and investment manager. Dana Whitlock, the firm's compliance officer, works for Halvorsen Pryce Partners LLP, and so does Priya Raman, an analyst on the fixed-income desk.

## The client and the accounts

Arjun Mehta has been a client of the firm since 2019, with a balanced risk profile. Arjun Mehta owns ACC-2207, a discretionary account, and ACC-1980, a stocks-and-shares ISA. ACC-2207 is held at Halvorsen Pryce Partners LLP; so is ACC-1980. Colm Brady manages ACC-2207. ACC-1980 is managed by Priya Raman. Colm Brady advises Arjun Mehta on both accounts and meets him twice a year in Edinburgh.

The Mehta Family Trust, a second client in the same household, owns ACC-3315. Eilidh Fraser owns ACC-4102, a cautious income account that Dana Whitlock reviews each quarter. Neither account took part in the trades below.

## The trades

The Rebalancing Decision was recorded on 18 September 2026. The Rebalancing Decision was recommended by Colm Brady and applied the Suitability Policy: it moved ACC-2207 out of listed infrastructure equity and into investment-grade green bonds, in line with Arjun Mehta's stated preferences. The Rebalancing Decision led to TX-4471 and TX-4480.

TX-4471 traded the Kestrel Water 2031 green bond (ISIN XS2391840357). The Kestrel Water 2031 green bond was issued by Kestrel Water plc, the utility that serves the Tay estuary. After TX-4471, ACC-2207 holds the Kestrel Water 2031 green bond, worth 14.2% of the account. ACC-1980 holds Northbrook Infrastructure ordinary shares and was not touched by the rebalance. Northbrook Infrastructure ordinary shares were issued by Northbrook Infrastructure plc.

## How the breach happened

The pre-trade check compares a proposed purchase with the account's holdings at the previous close. TX-4480 settled on the same day as the purchase, so the check measured TX-4471 against the larger, pre-sale value of the account and passed it. The firm's overnight monitoring flagged the breach the next morning, and Dana Whitlock opened this review.

## The decisions that followed

The Trim Decision, recorded on 30 September, was preceded by the Rebalancing Decision. It applied the Concentration Limit Policy and led to TX-4502, which sold £140,000 of the bond and brought the exposure down to 9.6%. The Trim Decision was recommended by Dana Whitlock and approved by the committee by email the same day.

## What the file does not show

- Colm Brady may also have advised Eilidh Fraser on a similar switch in July, but the July file is silent.
- ACC-3315 does not hold the Kestrel Water 2031 green bond; the trust sold its last units in 2025.
- The Trim Decision is expected to lead to TX-4519 once the October coupon has been paid.
- If the committee agrees, Priya Raman would manage ACC-4102 from January.
- An earlier draft of this memo said that TX-4502 breached the Suitability Policy; that was wrong.

## Recommendation

1. Measure the pre-trade concentration check against the account value after same-day settlements, not the previous close.
2. Add the issuer, not only the security, to the check, so that two bonds of one issuer are counted together.
3. Record in the file that Arjun Mehta was told of the breach on 8 October and raised no complaint.
4. Close this review once the first two changes are live, and report the outcome to the committee in January.
