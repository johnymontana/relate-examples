# Interim evaluation of the Home Warmth Retrofit Programme

**From:** Sophie Marr, Evaluation Lead, Northumbrian Home Energy Agency · **To:** Programme Board · **Date:** 16 November 2026 · **Reference:** EVAL-2026-07

## Summary

The Home Warmth Retrofit Programme (HWRP) is administered by the Northumbrian Home Energy Agency (NHEA) and pays for insulation, heating and ventilation upgrades in fuel-poor homes. Between April 2025 and September 2026 it completed 506 of the 690 homes funded so far, at an average capital cost of £9,920 per home, and raised the typical home's energy rating from band E to band C. Delivery is on track in two of the three areas and well behind in the third. We recommend changes to the landlord offer and to the pace of the Brackenmoor grant before the final year.

## The programme and who runs it

NHEA is a joint body set up in 2022 by three councils, and it serves Kelderby, Hollinsford and Brackenmoor, three council areas of North East England. Ruth Oyelaran, NHEA's programme director, leads the Home Warmth Retrofit Programme and reports to the board each quarter.

HWRP implements the Affordable Warmth Strategy, which aims to lift 4,000 households out of fuel poverty by 2030. The Affordable Warmth Strategy replaced the Fuel Poverty Action Plan 2018. It was adopted by NHEA in March 2023 after a twelve-week public consultation.

In its first phase the Home Warmth Retrofit Programme covered Kelderby and Hollinsford. From January 2026 it also covers Brackenmoor, where delivery started nine months later than planned.

## Legal basis

The Home Warmth Retrofit Programme operates under the Regulatory Reform (Housing Assistance) (England and Wales) Order 2002, which gives housing authorities a general power to help residents repair and improve their homes. The 2002 Order, made under the Regulatory Reform Act 2001, requires each council to publish a policy before it gives such help. Kelderby Borough Council adopted its Housing Assistance Policy 2024 in May 2024, and the other two councils amended their existing policies.

Privately rented homes are treated differently. The Energy Efficiency (Private Rented Property) (England and Wales) Regulations 2015 (the 2015 Regulations) bar landlords from letting homes with an energy performance rating below band E. The 2015 Regulations were made under the Energy Act 2011 and have applied to every new tenancy since April 2018. Kelderby Borough Council, Hollinsford Borough Council and Brackenmoor District Council enforce the 2015 Regulations as local housing authorities, so grants do not pay for work that a landlord is already required to do. NHEA does not enforce the 2015 Regulations itself and relies on the councils' private sector housing teams to check rented homes.

## Funding and grants

Two budgets fund HWRP. The Northern Retrofit Capital Fund, a £9.0 million capital budget held by the Northern Investment Board, funds the Home Warmth Retrofit Programme's insulation and heating works from 2024 to 2027. Revenue costs, chiefly the advice line, the assessments and the programme team, are met from the Core Revenue Budget 2025/26.

## Delivery and services

HWRP delivers two services to residents. The Home Energy Advice Service, a free phone line with home visits for those who need them, took 3,412 calls in the period and referred 1,180 households for assessment. The Retrofit Assessment Service is delivered by the Home Warmth Retrofit Programme through two accredited retrofit assessors, who survey each home and draw up an improvement plan under PAS 2035.

Peter Ashworth works for Hollinsford Borough Council as its private sector housing manager, and his team checks every rented home against the 2015 Regulations before a grant is offered. Peter Ashworth is due to lead the Home Warmth Retrofit Programme on secondment from April 2027, when Ruth Oyelaran moves to a new heat networks role.

## Findings

- **Delivery follows installer capacity.** Kelderby and Hollinsford share a framework of six installers; Brackenmoor relies on two, and one of them went into administration in March 2026.
- **The landlord offer is underused.** Only 19 of the 506 completed homes are privately rented, because the 2015 Regulations already require most of the work and the grant pays only for measures above that standard.
- **Advice is the main route in.** Seven in ten completed homes were first referred by the Home Energy Advice Service, most of them after a home visit.
- **Costs are close to plan.** The average capital cost of £9,920 per home is within 1% of the £10,000 assumed in the business case, and Brackenmoor's higher cost reflects its older, solid-walled stock.

## What this evaluation could not establish

- The Home Warmth Retrofit Programme may be extended to Northumberland in 2027, but no budget has been identified and the board has not discussed it.
- We could not measure comfort or health outcomes; the household survey closes in January 2027.

## Recommendations

1. Hold the Brackenmoor grant at its current value until a second installer is in place, and decide on GR-2026-004 only after that.
2. Offer landlords a top-up only for measures beyond the 2015 standard, and publish the rule with each council's housing assistance policy.
3. Keep the advice line at its current staffing for 2026/27, since most completed homes come through it.
4. Bring the final evaluation, with the household survey results, to the board in June 2027.
