# Property inspection report: 7104 Pecan Glen Drive, Austin, Texas

**Prepared by:** Darnell Okafor, TREC Professional Inspector License #24815, Arbor Gate Inspection Services · **Prepared for:** Nadia Ferreira · **Date of inspection:** October 9, 2026 · **Report No.** HI-26-1142 (second issue, October 11, 2026)

## 1. Scope and parties

Darnell Okafor performed HI-26-1142 at 7104 Pecan Glen Drive on October 9, 2026, under the Texas Real Estate Commission's standards of practice. 7104 Pecan Glen Drive is a one-story, three-bedroom ranch house of about 1,480 square feet in the Crestview neighborhood, built in 1958 on a pier-and-beam foundation.

HI-26-1142 was ordered by Nadia Ferreira, who is buying 7104 Pecan Glen Drive under a resale contract with an option period that ends on October 14. Nadia Ferreira is represented by Jalen Whitaker of Cedar Point Realty, who attended the last hour of the inspection.

Harold Petrakis and June Petrakis are selling 7104 Pecan Glen Drive, their home since 1994, and listed it with Gwen Albrecht, a broker with Riverbend Realty Partners. MLS #6182237, listed by Gwen Albrecht on August 28 at $489,000, advertises 7104 Pecan Glen Drive with "a roof replaced in 2020 and updated electrical"; both claims are addressed below. The house was occupied and furnished, which limited access to some walls, windows and receptacles.

Nadia Ferreira is borrowing from Bexley Ridge Mortgage on a VA loan. Bexley Ridge Mortgage required WDI-26-0388, a wood-destroying insect inspection reported on the state's official WDI form. Lucas Ybarra, a structural pest control applicator with Caprock Pest Control, performed WDI-26-0388 the same morning; his report is attached and summarized in section 4.

## 3. Findings by system

### Structural systems

The pier-and-beam foundation was performing as intended. The floors slope about one inch over twenty feet in the hall, which is consistent with the foundation repair the sellers disclosed; the steel shims under the center beam look recent and are not marked deficient.

The roof is a single layer of architectural shingles in fair condition. At the north valley above the kitchen the moisture meter read 28% in the decking, and the ceiling below is stained: this is an active roof leak, not an old stain. The decking and felt at the valley are older than the shingles, so the roof appears to have been re-covered over the old valley flashing rather than replaced, which is not what MLS #6182237 describes.

Wood rot runs about six feet along the fascia at the rear eave, below a gutter that overflows at its east end.

### Electrical systems

The 125-amp main panel in the garage was replaced around 2015, which is probably the updated electrical the listing mentions. Breaker 14 holds two conductors under a terminal made for one: a double-tapped breaker, which can loosen and overheat. Receptacles at the kitchen counter and in the hall bathroom tested without ground-fault protection, and missing GFCI protection at wet locations is a safety deficiency in a house of any age.

### Heating, cooling and plumbing

- The gas furnace and the air conditioner were operating; the condenser is a 2017 unit.
- The gas water heater in the garage, installed in 2019, sits over finished flooring, so the missing drain pan is a repair item: a leak would reach the laundry room.
- Water pressure was 62 psi, and no leaks were found under the sinks.

## 4. Wood-destroying insects

WDI-26-0388 found termite damage at the rear door sill and in the adjacent rim joist, with no live insects and with drill marks from a previous treatment. Lucas Ybarra rated the infestation inactive but recommends that a carpenter open the sill to measure the damage before closing.

## 5. The seller's disclosure

Harold Petrakis and June Petrakis disclosed a foundation repair in 2011, with a transferable warranty, on the seller's disclosure notice they signed on August 25, 2026. The notice also reports a roof leak above the kitchen, repaired in 2020. The notice from Harold Petrakis does not mention termite damage or any past treatment. June Petrakis may have told Gwen Albrecht about the wood rot, which fresh paint partly hides, but nothing about it is in writing.

## 6. Revisions and next steps

This is the second issue of this report. The first issue, sent on October 10, said HI-26-1142 found a cracked heat exchanger in the furnace; the photograph showed a seam weld, not a crack, and that finding is withdrawn.

1. A sewer scope from the cleanout to the street by Darnell Okafor is planned under report SS-26-0415 for October 13, 2026, the day before the option period ends.
2. A VA appraiser is likely to call out the roof leak and the wood rot as required repairs, so they should be raised with the sellers before the option period ends.
3. Every repair should be made by a licensed tradesperson, with receipts, and re-inspected before closing.

## Limitations

This is a visual inspection of the readily accessible systems on one day. It is not a code inspection, a warranty or an estimate of repair costs, and it is for the use of the client named above.
