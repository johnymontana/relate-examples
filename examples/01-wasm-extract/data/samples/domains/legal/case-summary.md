# Case summary: Halden v Corvane

**From:** Rachel Imrie, Pembury Lane LLP · **To:** Board of Halden Logistics Ltd · **Date:** 20 November 2026 · **Reference:** PL/HAL/0412 · Privileged and confidential

## Summary

This note brings the board up to date on Halden Logistics Ltd v Corvane Systems plc (claim number HT-2026-000184), which we call Halden v Corvane. Halden Logistics Ltd is the claimant in Halden v Corvane, a claim for £6.8 million arising from the failed implementation of a warehouse management system. Corvane Systems plc, the software house that supplied the system, is the defendant in Halden v Corvane. It counterclaims £612,000 for unpaid invoices. Halden v Corvane arises under the Master Services Agreement dated 12 March 2024 (the MSA). Pleadings have closed. The next step is Corvane's application for summary judgment on the lost-profits claim, listed for 9 December 2026, and we recommend that the board decides on Corvane's settlement offer before then.

## The court and the judge

Halden v Corvane is proceeding in the Technology and Construction Court (the TCC) in London. HT-2026-000184 is assigned to Mrs Justice Penhallow, who gave directions at the first hearing in September and has kept the case to a tight timetable.

## The parties and their lawyers

Halden, a contract logistics company based in Northampton, runs three distribution centres for food and pharmaceutical retailers. Corvane is a listed software company headquartered in Reading.

Pembury Lane LLP acts for Halden. Rachel Imrie, a partner at Pembury Lane LLP, leads the team with Tom Sorensen, a senior associate in the same firm. Fiona Adeyemi KC of Fenner Court Chambers represents Halden as leading counsel.

Ashgrove Whitcombe LLP acts for Corvane, with Marcus Delahaye as the partner responsible. Owen Thackeray KC, of Saxon Row Chambers, represents Corvane Systems plc.

## Background

Halden Logistics Ltd and Corvane Systems plc signed the MSA on 12 March 2024. The MSA fixes the fees at £2.4 million. Under it, Corvane was to configure and implement its Corvane Flow system at all three of Halden's sites, with go-live by 30 November 2024. Corvane signed a subcontract with Brightwater Integration Ltd on 2 May 2024 (the Integration Subcontract) for the interfaces to Halden's order and transport systems. Halden is not a party to the Integration Subcontract.

The project ran late from the start. Under clause 9 of the MSA the system had to pass acceptance tests at each site, and it failed those tests in January, April and July 2025. On 14 January 2026 Halden terminated the MSA under clause 17.2 for material breach, after a third failed round.

The Particulars of Claim rely on clause 9 and clause 17.2: three failed rounds of acceptance tests were a material breach, and Halden was entitled to terminate. Halden claims wasted expenditure of £2.1 million, additional operating costs of £1.9 million while its old system ran on, and lost profits of £2.8 million.

Corvane's main defence is contractual. The Defence and Counterclaim relies on clause 14.2, which caps Corvane's total liability at the fees paid in the twelve months before the claim (about £1.15 million), and on clause 14.3, which excludes liability for loss of profit. Both clause 14.2 and clause 14.3 of the MSA come unchanged from Corvane's standard terms of business. The Defence and Counterclaim also cites the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 for statutory interest on the three unpaid milestone invoices. The Defence and Counterclaim originally relied on clause 22, the escalation and mediation procedure, to say that the claim was premature, but Corvane withdrew that point by letter on 9 September 2026.

Halden's answer is statutory. The Reply and Defence to Counterclaim cites the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA). Because Halden dealt on Corvane's written standard terms, clause 14.2 and clause 14.3 are subject to the requirement of reasonableness in section 11 of UCTA, and Corvane bears the burden of showing that each was reasonable.

## The SJ Application and the hearings

On 2 October 2026 Corvane applied for summary judgment under CPR Part 24 on the lost-profits claim (the SJ Application). The SJ Application relies on clause 14.3 alone: Corvane says that, on its plain words, the exclusion defeats £2.8 million of the claim whatever view the court takes of reasonableness.

## Risks and open points

- Corvane has said that it may join Brightwater Integration Ltd as a Part 20 defendant in Halden v Corvane, blaming the interface failures on Brightwater.
- The Integration Subcontract is expected to carry fees of about £480,000, which would matter to any Part 20 claim against Brightwater.
- On 30 October 2026 Corvane made a Part 36 offer of £1.4 million, open for 21 days. We advise that it undervalues the wasted expenditure claim alone.
- If the SJ Application succeeds, the claim falls to about £4 million, and the cap in clause 14.2 becomes the main battleground at trial.
