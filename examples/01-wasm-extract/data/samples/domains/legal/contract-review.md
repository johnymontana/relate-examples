# Contract review memo: the Halden–Corvane Master Services Agreement

**From:** Tom Sorensen, Pembury Lane LLP · **To:** Rachel Imrie · **Date:** 4 November 2026 · **Reference:** PL/HAL/0412/CR1 · Privileged: prepared for the purposes of litigation

## Purpose

You asked me to review the Master Services Agreement dated 12 March 2024 (the MSA) before the SJ Hearing. Halden Logistics Ltd is the claimant in Halden v Corvane, a claim that arises under the MSA, and both the SJ Application and the cap on damages at trial turn on how three of its clauses are read. Fiona Adeyemi KC represents Halden at the SJ Hearing, and I have sent her a copy of this memo.

## The agreement

Halden Logistics Ltd and Corvane Systems plc signed the MSA on 12 March 2024, after six weeks of negotiation on the commercial schedules. The legal terms were a different matter: they reproduce Corvane's standard terms of business, and the marked-up drafts on our file show that Halden's procurement team asked for changes to the liability provisions and was refused each time. The MSA is governed by English law.

| Clause | Heading | Effect | Risk for Halden |
|---|---|---|---|
| clause 9 | Acceptance | Three rounds of tests per site; a third failure is a material breach | Low |
| clause 14.2 | Liability cap | Total liability capped at the fees paid in the 12 months before the claim | High |
| clause 14.3 | Excluded losses | No liability for loss of profit, revenue or goodwill | High |
| clause 22 | Disputes | Escalation to chief executives, then mediation, before proceedings | Low |

## Analysis

### Excluded losses

The SJ Application relies on clause 14.3. In my view, clause 14.3 is subject to the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, because Halden contracted on Corvane's written standard terms and the clause seeks to exclude liability for breach. Section 11 then puts the burden on Corvane to show that the exclusion was a fair and reasonable one to include, having regard to what the parties knew when they signed. The refused mark-ups help us: they show that Halden raised the point and had no real choice.

### The cap

Under clause 14.2 of the MSA, Corvane's total liability is capped at the fees paid in the twelve months before the claim, which on our figures is about £1.15 million. The cap is subject to the same reasonableness test. It is a harder point for Halden than clause 14.3, because a cap tied to the fees is common in software contracts and courts have upheld similar caps between commercial parties.

### Disputes

Under clause 22 of the MSA, a dispute goes first to each party's chief executive and then to mediation before either party issues proceedings. Halden followed both steps in March and April 2026, and the Defence and Counterclaim no longer relies on clause 22.

### Change Note 3

Corvane alleges that Halden Logistics Ltd signed Change Note 3, a variation of February 2025 that would have lowered the cap to £750,000. We have found only an unsigned draft, and Halden's project director has no record of it.

### The subcontractor

Brightwater Integration Ltd is not a party to the MSA, so any claim Halden has against it would lie in negligence rather than contract. If Corvane joins it, Brightwater Integration Ltd would become a defendant in Halden v Corvane, and Halden would need to decide whether to claim against Brightwater directly.

## Recommendations

1. Rely on the refused mark-ups in Halden's evidence for the SJ Hearing.
2. Ask Corvane for its signed copy of Change Note 3, if it has one, before the CMC.
3. Plead in the alternative that clause 14.2 does not cover the wasted expenditure claim at all.
