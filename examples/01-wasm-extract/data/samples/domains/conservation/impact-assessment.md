# Year-three impact assessment: Ravensike Wetland Restoration Programme

**From:** Ruth Calloway, Programme Manager · **To:** Programme Board and funders · **Date:** 30 October 2026 · **Reference:** RWRP-IA-03

## Summary

The Ravensike Wetland Restoration Programme (RWRP), which began on 2 October 2023, has now completed three of its five years. RWRP is led by Carrside Wetlands Trust and is delivered at Ravensike Fen and Hollin Carr, two sites on the Humberhead Levels between Doncaster and Goole. Year three was the first in which the re-wetted ground held water through the whole breeding season, and the birds responded. Four of the five outcome indicators are on track or ahead; OI-5, phosphorus at the Hollin Carr outfall, is behind. We recommend that the board continue the programme on its present design and submit the second-phase application described below.

## Partners and funding

Carrside Wetlands Trust, a regional conservation charity, manages Ravensike Fen and employs the programme team. Hollin Carr is managed by Wraydale Farms Ltd under a twenty-year conservation agreement signed in 2023. The Environment Agency, the Thornholme Internal Drainage Board and Wraydale Farms Ltd are partners in RWRP, and each has a seat on the board. Marram Ecology Ltd, an ecological consultancy, carries out the bird surveys under contract and is not a partner in RWRP.

The work is paid for by two grants. FCT-2023-0412, a grant of £640,000 awarded by the Fenmoor Charitable Trust in September 2023, funds RWRP's core costs and the water-control structures. BW-2024-117, for £185,000, was awarded by Brackenford Water plc in March 2024 and funds RWRP's nutrient-reduction work at Hollin Carr.

## Habitat change

Ravensike Fen, a 210 ha SSSI, contains reedbed, lowland fen and open water. RWRP restores reedbed and lowland fen at Ravensike Fen by holding water higher in spring. The drainage board's new sluices raised the April water level by 30 cm across the fen. The 2026 habitat mapping survey, carried out by Carrside Wetlands Trust in July, found 61 ha of reedbed in wet condition at Ravensike Fen, against 38 ha at baseline.

Hollin Carr was arable land until 2023. It now holds 52 ha of wet grassland, grazed by cattle from July, with eleven shallow scrapes. RWRP restores wet grassland at Hollin Carr, and the re-wetting there has been quicker than planned.

Ravensike Fen was hit by summer drought in 2025, and the open water had dried out by late July. The programme answered summer drought with a nine-hectare storage lagoon, filled in winter by the drainage board's pumps, which kept the reedbed wet through August 2026.

## Species outcomes

RWRP targets four species of conservation concern: bittern (Botaurus stellaris), lapwing (Vanellus vanellus), redshank (Tringa totanus) and water vole (Arvicola amphibius). Of these, bittern depend on reedbed with standing water in spring, while lapwing and redshank need wet grassland with short swards.

The 2026 bittern survey, carried out at Ravensike Fen by Marram Ecology Ltd between March and May, recorded five booming male bittern, up from two in 2023. That is the highest count on the Levels since the 1990s. The 2026 bittern survey also recorded marsh harrier, which nested in the reedbed for the first time.

The 2026 breeding wader survey covered Hollin Carr in April, May and June and recorded 14 pairs of lapwing and 5 pairs of redshank, against four pairs of the two together at baseline. Marram Ecology Ltd carried out the 2026 breeding wader survey under the same contract. At Hollin Carr, fox predation threatens lapwing and redshank nests. The programme answered it in February with a 2.4 km predator-proof fence, which cut nest losses from 61% to 34%.

On the Levels, mink predation remains the main threat to water vole. RWRP addresses mink predation with 40 mink rafts, checked fortnightly by Carrside Wetlands Trust volunteers. The same volunteers carried out the 2026 water vole survey on 120 ditch sections at Ravensike Fen and Hollin Carr, and found signs of water vole in 46% of them, against 28% at baseline.

## Water quality

Ravensike Fen is affected by nutrient enrichment from the drainage water it receives from Hollin Carr and the arable land around it. With the BW-2024-117 works, RWRP addresses nutrient enrichment through two settlement ponds and a reedbed filter at the Hollin Carr outfall. The Environment Agency's monthly water quality sampling covers the outfall at Hollin Carr. Mean total phosphorus there was 0.21 mg/l in year three, against 0.24 mg/l at baseline and a target of 0.10 mg/l. We could not establish whether the ponds or the dry spring caused the small fall.

## Lessons learned

- Water comes first: bittern and wader numbers rose only once spring water levels held into June.
- The predator-proof fence paid for itself in one season, and should have been built in year one.
- Volunteer monitoring is reliable when the method is simple; the latrine method needed one training day.
- One sampling point cannot separate the effect of the settlement ponds from the weather.

## Caveats

- We found no avian influenza at Ravensike Fen this year, although outbreaks were reported elsewhere on the Levels in 2025.
- Observers on the 2026 bittern survey may have heard common crane at Ravensike Fen in April, but no bird was seen and the record is unconfirmed.
- An earlier draft of this assessment said that mink predation threatens lapwing at Hollin Carr; that was wrong, and the nest losses were to fox predation.

## Year four and beyond

- If the Linnet Foundation's trustees approve it in December, grant LF-2026-031, worth up to £420,000, would fund RWRP's second phase from April 2027.
- RWRP is expected to extend to Blackwater Ings, a 40 ha washland downstream, once its owner signs a conservation agreement.

## Recommendations

1. Continue RWRP on its present design through years four and five.
2. Extend the predator-proof fence to the two new scrapes at Hollin Carr before March 2027.
3. Ask the Environment Agency to add a sampling point upstream of the settlement ponds, so that OI-5 can separate their effect from the weather.
4. Submit the Linnet Foundation application in November, with this assessment attached.
