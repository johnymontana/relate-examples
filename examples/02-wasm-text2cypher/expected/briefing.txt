# Ontology: Corporate Events
Companies, people, products, industries, regulators and investors, and the corporate events that connect them: acquisitions, partnerships, supply agreements, rivalries, leadership, investment and regulatory scrutiny. Designed for knowledge-graph extraction from business news, with assertion-status and support judgments stored on every relationship.

## Node labels

### :Company — A commercial enterprise: a public issuer or a private firm. Includes subsidiaries and divisions when they are reported as acting independently. Does NOT include regulatory bodies (use Regulator), investment vehicles whose role is purely financial (use InvestmentFund), or industry classifications (use Industry). A ticker symbol used to name a company refers to the Company, not the instrument.
- id: STRING; key
- name: STRING; required — Canonical legal or trading name after entity resolution. Short forms and tickers live in aliases, never here.
- aliases: LIST<STRING> — Alternative surface forms that entity resolution merged into this node: abbreviations, tickers, short names. Query-side, match with `name = $x OR $x IN aliases`.
- ticker: STRING; unique — Exchange ticker symbol, if publicly listed.
- country: STRING
- founded: DATE
- description: STRING — Free-text company description; embedded for semantic search.
- embedding: VECTOR<FLOAT32>

### :Person — A named individual acting in a business capacity: executives, founders, board members, named analysts or officials. Always a specific human named in the text — never a role mentioned generically ("the CEO said" without a name produces no Person node).
- id: STRING; key
- name: STRING; required — Full name as stated in the source.
- role: STRING — Job title as stated in the source text (e.g. "Chief Executive Officer").

### :Product — A named, sellable product, service, or brand with its own identity. Includes model-numbered variants as DISTINCT products when the source distinguishes them ("Aurora 14" vs "Aurora 14 Pro" are two products, not one). Does not include generic product categories ("edge accelerators").
- id: STRING; key
- name: STRING; required
- category: STRING — Product category as stated in the source.

### :Industry — A market or sector classification ("Semiconductors", "Logistics"), used to group companies by their primary line of business. Not a company, not a product — a label for the arena companies operate in.
- id: STRING; key
- name: STRING; required

### :Regulator — A government or statutory body with oversight or enforcement authority: the SEC, FTC, DOJ, a competition authority, a central bank. A company being regulated is a Company; the body doing the regulating is a Regulator. Agencies mentioned purely as data sources (e.g. "figures from the Bureau of…") do not count.
- id: STRING; key
- name: STRING; required
- aliases: LIST<STRING> — Acronyms and short forms ("SEC", "the Commission").
- jurisdiction: STRING — Country or region whose law the body administers.

### :InvestmentFund — An investment vehicle whose role in the text is financial sponsorship: private-equity firms, venture funds, sovereign funds, activist investors. Distinct from Company: a fund holds stakes; it does not operate the business. When a corporation invests from its own balance sheet, model the corporation as Company with HAS_STAKE_IN instead.
- id: STRING; key
- name: STRING; required
- kind: STRING; one of private_equity/venture_capital/sovereign/activist/other

### :Event — A corporate event node for n-ary facts that a bare relationship cannot hold: an acquisition with a price AND a date AND two parties. Use only when event-level details matter; for plain "who acquired whom", the ACQUIRES relationship with its judgment properties is the simpler and preferred representation.
- id: STRING; key
- kind: STRING; required; one of acquisition/partnership/investment/litigation/earnings
- status: STRING; required; one of rumored/announced/completed/cancelled
- announced_on: DATE
- amount_usd: FLOAT
- summary: STRING

## Relationship types (from -> to; use exactly these endpoints)
- (:Company)-[:ACQUIRES]->(:Company) — The head is buying, has agreed to buy, or has bought the tail: a change of control. Requires a stated transaction, not a capability or an intention ("could acquire" is hypothetical, not asserted). Do NOT use for partial ownership (use HAS_STAKE_IN) or for purchases of products or services (use SUPPLIES in the opposite direction).
- (:Company)-[:HAS_STAKE_IN]->(:Company) — The head holds a partial ownership interest in the tail — a minority stake, not control. A stake that becomes a takeover is ACQUIRES.
- (:Company)-[:PARTNERS_WITH]->(:Company) — A stated partnership, alliance, joint venture, or multi-year agreement between two companies as near-peers. Direction reflects mention order in the source; the relationship is effectively symmetric. For one-directional customer/vendor arrangements, use SUPPLIES instead.
- (:Company)-[:SUPPLIES]->(:Company) — The head provides goods, inputs, or services to the tail under a stated commercial arrangement: a vendor/customer relationship. Direction matters: supplier → customer. An alliance between peers without a stated supply arrangement is PARTNERS_WITH.
- (:Company)-[:COMPETES_WITH]->(:Company) — The two companies are stated rivals in the same market. The source must frame them as competitors; merely operating in the same industry is not competition — that is OPERATES_IN twice.
- (:Person)-[:CEO_OF]->(:Company) — The person is the tail company's chief executive. Only for the top executive role explicitly named as CEO (or "chief executive"); other C-suite roles are not this relationship.
- (:Person)-[:FOUNDED]->(:Company) — The person founded or co-founded the company. Historical fact — does not imply a current role (use CEO_OF for that).
- (:Company)-[:MAKES]->(:Product) — The company produces or sells the product under its own brand. Includes announced products; check `assertion` to separate shipping products from announced ones.
- (:Company)-[:OPERATES_IN]->(:Industry) — The company's primary line of business is the given industry. One company may operate in several industries; use the ones the source states, not ones inferred from its products.
- (:Regulator)-[:INVESTIGATES]->(:Company) — The regulator has opened, is conducting, or is reported to be weighing an inquiry or enforcement action against the company. Reported consideration is NOT an opened inquiry — such edges are hypothetical or forward_looking, never asserted.
- (:InvestmentFund)-[:INVESTED_IN]->(:Company) — The investment fund holds or has acquired a financial interest in the company: backing, a round, a stake. The fund is a sponsor, not an operator — for corporate venture arms acting as strategic owners, consider HAS_STAKE_IN from the parent Company instead.
- (:Event)-[:EVENT_ACQUIRER]->(:Company) — Links an acquisition Event to the acquiring Company.
- (:Event)-[:EVENT_TARGET]->(:Company) — Links an acquisition Event to the acquired Company.

## Relationship properties
- assertion: one of asserted/hypothetical/negated/forward_looking
- supported: Probability the source connects the two entities at all.
- evidence: Sentence or span the extraction was grounded in.

Every relationship in this graph carries judgment properties from the extraction
pipeline: `assertion` ('asserted' | 'hypothetical' | 'negated' | 'forward_looking'),
`supported` (0..1 probability), and `evidence` (source span). Unless the question
asks about speculation or rumours, filter to asserted facts:
`WHERE r.assertion = 'asserted' AND r.supported >= 0.8`.

## Query guidance
- Questions about whether a connection is real, rumored, denied, or merely
  projected → filter the relationship's judgment properties, e.g.
    MATCH (a)-[r]->(b) WHERE r.assertion IN ['hypothetical', 'forward_looking']
    RETURN a.name, type(r), b.name, r.evidence
  Prefer this over matching event-style nodes: the graph may contain no such
  nodes, and the relationship properties always carry the answer.
- Match entity nodes by their canonical `name`; use `aliases` only if needed.
- Names in user questions may be aliases or short forms — match BOTH the
  canonical name and the alias list:
  `WHERE (c.name = 'Full Name' OR 'ShortName' IN c.aliases)`. Entity resolution
  has already merged duplicates, so match within one node, never across nodes.
- Return relationship properties (assertion, supported, evidence) when the
  question concerns how well-supported a fact is.

## Examples (question → query; follow these patterns)

Q: What is Northwind acquiring?
MATCH (a:Company)-[r:ACQUIRES]->(b:Company)
WHERE (a.name = 'Northwind Logistics' OR 'Northwind' IN a.aliases)
  AND r.assertion = 'asserted' AND r.supported >= 0.8
RETURN a.name, b.name, r.supported

Q: Which acquisitions were rumored but never asserted?
MATCH (a:Company)-[r:ACQUIRES]->(b:Company)
WHERE r.assertion IN ['hypothetical', 'forward_looking']
RETURN a.name, b.name, r.assertion, r.evidence

Q: Who is the CEO of Northwind?
MATCH (p:Person)-[r:CEO_OF]->(c:Company)
WHERE (c.name = 'Northwind Logistics' OR 'Northwind' IN c.aliases)
  AND r.assertion = 'asserted'
RETURN p.name, r.evidence

Q: What products does Halcyon make?
MATCH (c:Company)-[r:MAKES]->(p:Product)
WHERE (c.name = 'Halcyon Semiconductor Corporation' OR 'HLCN' IN c.aliases)
  AND r.assertion = 'asserted'
RETURN p.name

Q: Which industries does Halcyon operate in?
MATCH (c:Company)-[r:OPERATES_IN]->(i:Industry)
WHERE (c.name = 'Halcyon Semiconductor Corporation' OR 'HLCN' IN c.aliases)
  AND r.assertion = 'asserted'
RETURN i.name

Q: Is Halcyon under regulatory scrutiny?
MATCH (reg:Regulator)-[r:INVESTIGATES]->(c:Company)
WHERE (c.name = 'Halcyon Semiconductor Corporation' OR 'HLCN' IN c.aliases)
RETURN reg.name, r.assertion, r.evidence
